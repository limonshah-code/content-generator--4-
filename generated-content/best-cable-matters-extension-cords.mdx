## What to Look for Before Buying the Best Cable Matters Extension Cords

### Find Your Need - Compatibility and use case considerations
Before you make a purchase, determine whether your device requires a grounded 3-prong plug or a simple 2-prong polarized plug. Using a 2-prong extension for a 3-prong device is dangerous and should never be done. Also, consider the environment; outdoor use requires cords with special water-resistant jackets and higher durability than standard indoor versions. If you are managing a server room, specialized C13/C14 cables are the only way to go.

### Budget - Setting realistic price expectations ($10-$35 ranges)
Extension cords from Cable Matters are generally affordable, but the price varies based on length, wire gauge, and special features like rotating plugs or high-speed data capabilities. A standard 2-pack of indoor cords usually falls in the $10 to $15 range. High-performance USB4 or heavy-duty 14 AWG cords can go up to $30 or $35. Investing a few extra dollars in a thicker gauge is always a wise choice for long-term safety.

### Key Features - Most important technical specifications
The most critical specification is the wire gauge (AWG). A lower number like 14 AWG indicates a thicker wire capable of carrying more current than 16 AWG or 18 AWG. Also, look for the amperage rating; most household devices need 13A, but heavy tools require 15A. Features like tamper guards for safety and low-profile flat plugs for tight spaces are also high-priority items to consider.

### Quality & Durability - What to look for in construction
Check for ETL or UL listings, which ensure the product has been tested for safety. Look at the quality of the molded connectors; they should feel solid and have integrated strain relief to prevent the wire from pulling out. The jacket material is also important; SJT jackets are common for power cords, while SPT-2 is standard for lighter-duty lamp cords. A more flexible jacket is easier to work with but should still feel substantial.

### Brand Reputation - Importance of established manufacturers
Cable Matters is well-regarded for providing reliable, certified products at a competitive price. Buying from an established brand ensures that the specifications listed are accurate. Non-branded or extremely cheap cords may use copper-clad aluminum instead of pure copper, which increases the risk of overheating. Sticking with a known brand gives you access to better customer support and verified safety standards.

### Portability/Size - Considerations for space and storage
If you plan to use an extension cord for travel, a short 3-foot or 6-foot version is much easier to pack. For home use, choosing the exact length you need prevents clutter. Coiling long cords can sometimes cause heat buildup if they are under heavy load, so picking the right size for the job is both a matter of aesthetics and safety.

## What Is the Best Way to Choose cable matters extension cords?

Choosing the best power solution involves a systematic approach to ensure you are meeting both technical and safety requirements. Start by checking the power label on the device you want to plug in. Note the wattage and amperage requirements. Once you have that, follow these steps:

1. **Match the Prongs:** If your device has three prongs, you must use a three-prong extension cord to ensure it is properly grounded.
2. **Determine the Gauge:** For heavy appliances like fridges or space heaters, choose 14 AWG or 16 AWG. For lamps and chargers, 16 AWG or 18 AWG is sufficient.
3. **Measure the Distance:** Always choose the shortest cord that comfortably reaches your outlet. Longer cords have more electrical resistance, which can cause voltage drops.
4. **Consider the Environment:** If the cord will be exposed to moisture or sunlight, it must be rated for outdoor use.
5. **Check for Certifications:** Never buy a power cord that does not have an ETL or UL safety mark.
6. **Evaluate Plug Design:** If the outlet is behind a couch or bed, a flat-head or right-angle plug is a mandatory feature to prevent cord damage.

Avoid the common mistake of daisy-chaining multiple extension cords together, as this significantly increases the risk of fire. Instead, buy a single cord of the correct length. If you find yourself needing more than three outlets, consider a high-quality surge protector instead of a simple extension cord.
